What is the Poisson Distribution?

The Poisson distribution is a probability distribution that helps us answer questions like:

  • How many customers will visit my store in the next hour?
  • How many emails will I receive today?
  • How many meteor strikes will hit Earth this year?
  • How many typos are in a 10-page document?

Key Concept: Counting Rare Events Over Time or Space

Imagine you're running a coffee shop. On average, you get 3 customers per hour. But that's just an average! Sometimes you might get 1, sometimes 5, rarely 8 or more. The Poisson distribution tells you the probability of getting exactly k customers in that hour.

When to Use Poisson Distribution?

  • Events are independent - One customer arriving doesn't affect whether another arrives
  • Events happen at a constant average rate - You average 3 customers/hour consistently
  • You're counting discrete events - You can count 0, 1, 2, 3... customers (not 2.5!)
  • Two events can't happen at exactly the same instant - Each arrival is distinct

The Formula (Don't Worry, We'll Make It Simple!)

P(X = k) = (λk × e-λ) / k!

Where:

  • k = the number of events you're interested in (e.g., 5 customers)
  • λ (lambda) = the average rate (e.g., 3 customers per hour)
  • e = mathematical constant (≈ 2.718)
  • k! = k factorial (e.g., 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 120)

What Do You Notice?

  • Small λ (< 5): The distribution is right-skewed (tail on the right). Most values cluster near zero.
  • Medium λ (5-10): The distribution becomes more symmetric and bell-shaped.
  • Large λ (> 10): The distribution looks almost like a normal (bell) curve!
  • The mean equals the variance: Both equal λ. This is unique to the Poisson distribution!

Special Properties of the Poisson Distribution

1. Mean = Variance = λ

This is unique! If the average is 5, the variance is also 5. This makes calculations simple and helps identify if data follows a Poisson distribution.

2. Additive Property

If you have two independent Poisson processes, you can add them! If store A gets 3 customers/hour and store B gets 5 customers/hour, the total follows Poisson(8).

3. Approximates Binomial Distribution

When you have many trials (n) with a small probability (p), the Binomial distribution approximates a Poisson distribution with λ = n × p.

4. Memoryless for Arrival Times

The time between events follows an exponential distribution. The fact that no customer arrived in the last 5 minutes doesn't change the probability of one arriving in the next 5 minutes.

Shape Characteristics

  • Always discrete: You can only have whole number outcomes (0, 1, 2, 3...)
  • Always non-negative: Probabilities are always ≥ 0
  • Unlimited upper bound: Theoretically, k can go to infinity (but probabilities get tiny)
  • Mode (most likely value): Usually floor(λ) or floor(λ) - 1
  • Skewness decreases as λ increases: Becomes more symmetric with larger λ
